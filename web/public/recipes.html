Nutristatic 9000 Recipes

Every line is a link — click it to run. The point of the language is that these combine: a category, a shape, a meaning and an extraction are all just constraints, and the intersection is what solves a clue. The usage guide explains each piece.

All of these run over the network, without downloading anything. That was not true until recently: the ones that sift finished matches — {palindrome:…}, {compound …}, {reversible:…} — have to read a great deal of the index before the first match survives the sieve, and streamed a block at a time that spent the whole transfer budget before finding anything. Each index now ships with its highest-scoring entries as a small separate file, and every recipe here is answered out of that in well under a second. Past where it reaches, the full search takes over as before.

Cryptograms

A substitution cipher preserves the letter pattern of its text, so a cryptogram is a search: everything with that pattern under a one-to-one mapping, most plausible first, the recovered key beside each answer.

Solve a cryptogram word
MURMUR, TOMTOM — everything with the ciphertext's letter pattern under a one-to-one key, most common first, the key beside each answer.
{iso:zyxzyx}
Solve a two-word cryptogram
ALL TOO, SEE ALL, OFF ALL — one consistent key across both words.
{iso:utt qrr}
A cryptogram that decodes to a bird
HOOPOE is the only bird shaped a·bb·c·b·d. The pattern search and the category share the work.
{iso:abbcbd}&{kind:bird}

Captures and relations

{=name:…} remembers the span it matched; {eq}, {rev} and {shift} relate two named spans inside the pattern they wrap.

Two words, one the mirror of the other
SAW WAS. {=a:…} names a span; {rev a,b:…} demands the second reads as the first reversed.
{rev a,b:{=a:A{3,5}} {=b:A{3,5}}}
A word said twice
ROOT ROOT, THAT THAT — the same span, captured and demanded equal.
{eq a,b:{=a:A{4}} {=b:A{4}}}
Two words that are Caesar shifts of each other
THE and MAX are shift 19 apart; the shift found is reported. Fix it with {shift13 a,b:…} for exact ROT13 pairs.
{shift a,b:{=a:A{3}} {=b:A{3}}}

Several at once

The constructs are not a menu of separate tools: they compose, and a clue usually needs two or three of them pulling against each other. Each of these stacks features that work at different stages — the pattern prunes the walk, a construct intersects with it, a predicate sifts what survives.

Eight letters, one syllable
STRENGTH, STRAIGHT, THOUGHTS. The pattern bounds the length; the pronouncing dictionary decides the rest.
{syllables=1:A{8}}
Nine letters, spellable in chemical symbols, no letter twice
BIOGRAPHY, SINGAPORE, CHAMPIONS. Three constructs stacked: one restricts the spelling, one the letters, one the length.
{distinct:{elements:A{9}}}
Seven letters, no E, letter values over 90
THROUGH, HISTORY, STATION. A negation and a counter narrowing each other — E is the commonest letter, and dropping it while demanding a high total leaves the back of the alphabet.
A{7}&!.*e.*&{sum>90:A*}
A six-letter palindrome of two syllables
HANNAH. Two checks on the finished match, each of which would be hopeless as an automaton.
{palindrome:{syllables=2:A{6}}}
Nine letters, three syllables, stress on the middle one
SEPTEMBER, ACCORDING, AUSTRALIA. The shape of a word said aloud, with the length pinned.
{stress 010:{syllables=3:A{9}}}
A palindromic bird
TIT, and nothing else. WordNet supplies the birds; the palindrome check reads each one backwards.
{palindrome:{kind:bird}}
Eight letters, splits into two words, no letter repeated
TOWNSHIP is TOWN·SHIP, SQUADRON is SQUAD·RON. The split is looked up in the corpus while the pattern is still being walked.
{compound 2:{distinct:A{8}}}
A country with one letter changed, and the changed letter is O
IRON from IRAN, CHINO from CHINA, JORDON from JORDAN. The letters in brackets say what an edit may put in — the same (set) notation {count(e)=2:…} uses — so it works whether or not the country already had an O.
{subst1(o):{list:countries}}
A country with an E inserted somewhere
The bracket restricts an insertion the same way it restricts a substitution.
{add1(e):{list:countries}}
A word that rearranges a country
SERIAL from ISRAEL, PANEL from NEPAL, ANALOG from ANGOLA, ENEMY from YEMEN. The one it came from is shown beside each answer. Angle brackets rearrange the letters you write out, so they cannot rearrange a set — this asks the question of each finished match instead, and the argument may be any pattern with few enough matches to list.
{anagram countries:A{6}}
...and it may land in more than one word
IS REAL from ISRAEL, WAR AND from RWANDA, A VITAL from LATVIA. The letters are what matter, not where the spaces fall.
{anagram countries:A* A*}
A word that rearranges a bird
GARDEN from GANDER, SECTOR from SCOTER, DRIVEN from VERDIN. The argument is any pattern with few enough matches to list out, not just a named list — so anything that names a set can be rearranged.
{anagram {kind:bird}:A{6}}
A word that rearranges another word
BEATS and BATES from BEAST, SILENT from LISTEN. The argument may be a list name, a single word, or any pattern with few enough matches to list out — and the word itself is never offered back.
{anagram beast:A*}
A zodiac sign hiding inside a longer word
LIBRARY hides LIBRA. Any list drops into the same shape: .*{list:greek}.* finds hidden Greek letters.
.*{list:zodiac}.*&A{7,9}
A bird hiding after the first letter
WHEN hides a HEN. The bird category is an ordinary pattern piece, so letters may sit around it.
A+{kind:bird}A*
An insect whose reversal is also a word
GNAT ← TANG. The category feeds the walk; the reversal is asked of each survivor.
{reversible:{kind:insect}}
An anagram of an NBA team
SENT and NEST from NETS, HATE from HEAT — the team it came from is shown.
{anagram nbateams:A{4,6}}
An NBA team with one letter deleted
SUN, NET, KING — the pluralless teams fall out on their own.
{del1:{list:nbateams}}
Ends in -TION and is spellable in chemical symbols
SECTION is S·E·C·Ti·O·N; the suffix walk is served by the reverse sidecar.
{elements:A*tion}
Closest in meaning first, keep everything
ROYAL, then QUEEN — the tilde boosts instead of filtering, so the rest of the five-letter words still follow, just later. Quoted so the shape is one word.
{~near:royal}&"A{5}"
Everything within two edits of PUZZLE, nearest first
PUZZLE exact, then one edit, then two — a graded stream rather than a cutoff.
{edit:puzzle}

The deep end

Five that use most of the machine at once. Nothing here is a special mode — every one is ordinary constructs composed, which is the point.

Drop a letter from a country, anagram what's left — get a bird
CRANE is FRANCE without its F, rearranged; GANDER is GRENADA less an A; MOA is OMAN missing its N. Four layers deep: a curated list, inside an edit, inside an anagram predicate, intersected with a WordNet category — and each answer shows the intermediate it came from.
{anagram {del1:{list:countries}}:{kind:bird}}
The one-dollar bird
T+U+R+K+E+Y = 20+21+18+11+5+25 = exactly 100. The classic "dollar word" hunt, restricted to an entire taxonomy at once — STARLING and MERGANSER land on the same total.
{sum=100:{kind:bird}}
A tool, followed by itself in the mirror
SAW WAS, RAM MAR, TAP PAT. The capture {=a:…} may wrap any pattern — here a whole WordNet category — and {rev a,b:…} then demands the second word read the first one backwards. Wordplay discovered, not looked up.
{rev a,b:{=a:{kind:tool}} {=b:A{3}}}
A word that ends with its own beginning, reversed
REGISTER opens RE and closes ER; SERVICES opens SER and closes RES. Both captures live inside a single word with anything allowed between them — the relation machinery doesn't care where the spans sit.
{rev a,b:{=a:A{2,3}}A*{=b:A{2,3}}}&A{6,10}
Every bird — but the ones rhyming with DAY come first
JAY leads, and nothing is thrown away: the tilde makes rhyming a boost instead of a filter, so the rest of the birds still follow in frequency order. For when you suspect a rhyme but wouldn't bet the puzzle on it.
{~rhyme:day}&{kind:bird}&A{3,5}
